# Post hoc watermark localisation routes

Scope note: ~25 web calls, time-boxed. "Cited findings" were read from the linked page in this session (through a summarising fetch tool, so table numbers should be spot-checked before being quoted in a paper). Anything from memory is confined to "Gaps / unverified recall". Figures-only numbers could not be read and are marked as such.

## 1. Localisation-capable watermark papers: what makes localisation work, and how small can regions be?

### Takeaway
Every method that localises well was trained for it with splicing/mask augmentation and a dedicated dense head or dedicated segmentation network on the image (WAM: per-pixel detect+bits; MaskMark: a separate U2-Net localiser). The only benchmark that puts PixelSeal and TrustMark in a local setting (Sept 2026) does not attempt pixel localisation with them at all, and shows global decoding of PixelSeal collapses for regions of 0-3% area and that generative edits/outpainting drive payload recovery to chance.

### Cited Findings
- **"What Breaks Local Watermarks?"** (arXiv 2609.16832, 15 Sep 2026; Yao, ..., Juarez) benchmarks MaskWM (MaskMark), WAM, OmniGuard, TrustMark-Q (100 bits, ECC off) and PixelSeal (256 bits) over 55 transformations. It states "PixelSeal does not provide spatial output"; PixelSeal is made local by multiplying the full-image residual by the mask and decoding with the whole-image decoder. Localisation IoU is reported only for MaskWM and WAM. — [arXiv 2609.16832](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16832)
- Same benchmark, localisation IoU: clean MaskWM 0.902 vs WAM 0.293; direct inpainting of the watermark region MaskWM 0.779, WAM 0.906; direct splicing MaskWM 0.673, WAM 0.701; alignment changes MaskWM 0.741 (0.848 with SyncSeal), WAM 0.085 (0.192 with SyncSeal). — [arXiv 2609.16832](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16832)
- Same benchmark, region-size effect (COCO, post-transformation): IoU rises with region size from 0.634 to 0.825 for MaskWM and from 0.052 to 0.359 for WAM. "Evidence score" for PixelSeal is 0.36 in the 0-3% area bin and 27.42 in the 40-85% bin; MaskWM 3.84 -> 9.71. Mean region area in their data: COCO 15.22%, SA-1B 12.50%, DIV2K/MIRFLICKR 8.80%. — [arXiv 2609.16832](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16832)
- Same benchmark, generative edits: "geometric misalignment and generative local edits, such as inpainting and outpainting, can completely impair payload recovery"; "The two outpainting transformations also bring payload recovery down to random guessing". An "outside-restored" control (pixels outside the edit mask reset to the original watermarked values) restores MaskWM evidence to 14.62-17.45 across overlap bins; the authors attribute the loss to whole-image reconstruction by the Stable Diffusion inpainting pipeline. FLUX is not mentioned. — [arXiv 2609.16832](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16832)
- Benchmark code announced ("will be available") at github.com/garandorinc/markbench. — [arXiv 2609.16832](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16832)
- The benchmark says TrustMark is localised with "TrustMark's region detector [which] first predicts bounding boxes from the received image", then crop + resize + decode (cites Bui et al., ICCV 2025). No detector accuracy given. — [arXiv 2609.16832](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16832); the adobe/trustmark README mentions no region detector, only `encode`, `decode` (returns `wm_secret, wm_present, wm_schema`), `remove_watermark`, model types Q and P — [adobe/trustmark](https://github.com/adobe/trustmark). Conflict unresolved (see Gaps).
- **Watermark Anything (WAM)** (arXiv 2411.07231, ICLR 2025): extractor outputs per pixel a detection score + 32 bits; trained at 256x256 by splicing watermarked and original images with random masks (rectangles, irregular, segmentation maps) followed by common edits; phase 2 adds JND attenuation and multiple messages. For high-res, extraction resizes to 256x256. Reported: ~31/32 bits from a single 10% area; ~25/32 bits when the region is 10% of a 25% crop (~2.5% of pixels); Table 2 COCO inpainting TPR 97.9% / bit acc 99.2%, splicing TPR 100% / bit acc 95.3%; localisation degrades when ~95% of the image is watermarked (everything labelled watermarked). mIoU-vs-area is figures only. — [arXiv 2411.07231](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.07231)
- **MaskMark / "Mask Image Watermarking"** (arXiv 2504.12739, 2025): decoder = U2-Net mask predictor on the image, then U-Net+CNN bit extractor on the masked image; trained with LaMa-style masks (full, rectangle, irregular, COCO segments) on COCO at 256x256, 100k steps, 20 h on one A6000; evaluated at 512x512 with TrustMark-style resolution scaling. Claims "nearly 100% extraction accuracy even when only 5% of the image carries watermark signals"; IoU per watermark ratio only in figures (text: best "across nearly all watermark ratios", WAM "noticeable performance gap", EditGuard "significantly worse"). Fine-tuning 20k steps with VAE distortions (SD v1-4, Bmshj18, Cheng20) gives "near 100%" extraction against VAE attacks. Code: https://github.com/hurunyi/MaskMark — [arXiv 2504.12739](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12739v2)
- **"Where is the Watermark? Interpretable Watermark Detection at the Block Level"** (arXiv 2512.14994, WACV 2026): its own DWT red/green watermark; localisation = one-sided hypothesis test at 5% per non-overlapping 96x96 block (>=82 of 144 coefficients green). 92.5-95.2% of watermarked area flagged vs 16.8-19.5% of clean area flagged (i.e. a high per-block false-positive rate); no IoU reported. — [arXiv 2512.14994](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.14994)
- Other localisation papers surfaced by search but not read: GenPTW (arXiv 2504.19567), StableGuard (arXiv 2509.17993), RecoverMark (arXiv 2602.20618), SLICE (arXiv 2603.12749). All embed their own localisation-aware watermark. — [GenPTW](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.19567v2), [StableGuard](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.17993v1), [RecoverMark](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.20618), [SLICE](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12749)

### Inferences
- WAM's weak localisation in an independent benchmark (clean IoU 0.293; 0.052 for the smallest regions) matches the team's failed WAM fine-tune: WAM is not a strong starting point. The architecture that wins is MaskMark's: a full segmentation network (U2-Net) operating directly on image pixels, trained with rectangle/irregular/segment masks. Inference: a per-method U-Net/U2-Net trained from pixels at full 512 resolution on synthetic splices made with the real embedders is a better-supported route than heads on extractor features, especially for 1-6% regions where 1/16-1/32-resolution features are too coarse.
- Even the best trained-for-localisation model gets only ~0.63 IoU in its smallest-area bin after transformations, so mIoU 0.65 on 1-6% regions is near the published state of the art; large gains on small regions should not be expected from a head swap alone.
- The "outside-restored" result implies that in images that went through an SD-type pipeline the watermark is degraded everywhere, not only in the edited area. If the hackathon images went through a FLUX VAE, the "surviving" signal is a VAE-filtered version of the residual, and synthetic pre-training should include a real FLUX VAE round trip (speculation, but consistent with MaskMark's VAE fine-tuning result).

### Gaps
- EditGuard (CVPR 2024, arXiv 2312.08883), OmniGuard (arXiv 2412.01615), Robust-Wide, SSyncOA, TAG-WM, WOFA: not fetched this session; no verified numbers. Unverified recall: EditGuard/OmniGuard localise by hiding a fixed localisation image and comparing the recovered one with the known template, which requires their own embedder and does not transfer.
- IoU-vs-area curves for WAM and MaskMark are in figures; exact values for 1-5% areas not found.
- Whether adobe/trustmark really ships a region detector (benchmark says yes, README silent) is unresolved; check `python/CONFIG.md` and the ICCV 2025 version of the paper.

## 2. Turning a GLOBAL decoder into a localiser when the message is known

### Takeaway
Not found: no published method was located that localises with an unmodified global decoder (sliding-window decoding, known-message per-patch tests, matched filtering against a re-embedded residual, or mask optimisation through the decoder) for TrustMark, StegaStamp or VideoSeal/PixelSeal. The closest published practice is box-detect-then-crop-decode (TrustMark in the 2026 benchmark) and per-block hypothesis tests for a hand-crafted watermark.

### Cited Findings
- Targeted search for sliding-window / patch-wise known-message localisation with TrustMark, StegaStamp or VideoSeal returned no such paper; nearest hits were VidStamp (per-frame temporal localisation, accuracy 0.96, not spatial) and MaskMark. — [VidStamp arXiv 2505.01406](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.01406v2), [MaskMark](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12739v2)
- The 2026 benchmark, which explicitly picked methods "that either offer native localization or need minimal adaptation", did not attempt to derive masks from PixelSeal and excluded TrustMark from IoU because its output is a rectangle. — [arXiv 2609.16832](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16832)
- Block-level hypothesis testing with a known key works for a hand-crafted DWT watermark at 96x96 blocks but with ~17-19% of clean area falsely flagged at the 5% level. — [arXiv 2512.14994](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.14994)
- PixelSeal crop robustness (Fig. 8, Meta AI images, bit accuracy by crop parameter): 0.32 -> 0.58, 0.45 -> 0.81, 0.55 -> 0.90, 0.63 -> 0.95, 0.71 -> 0.98, 1.0 -> 1.00. — [arXiv 2512.16874](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16874)

### Inferences
- Known-message test power (arithmetic, not from a paper): with n known bits and per-bit accuracy a, z = (2a-1)*sqrt(n). PixelSeal 256 bits: a=0.58 -> z~2.6, a=0.81 -> z~9.9. TrustMark/StegaStamp 100 bits: a=0.60 -> z=2.0, a=0.70 -> z=4.0. So PixelSeal windows remain testable down to the "0.32" crop setting, whereas 100-bit methods need a >= 0.65-0.7 per window for a usable map.
- The PixelSeal crop parameters 0.32/0.45/0.55/0.63/0.71 equal sqrt(0.1 ... 0.5), so they are probably edge ratios for crops keeping 10-50% of the area (inference from the numbers; the fetched text did not state the unit). If so, a window covering 10% of the watermark's area still gives a weak (z~2.6) known-message signal, and a 20% window a strong one.
- Sliding-window known-message decoding is therefore plausible for PixelSeal with windows of roughly 45% edge of the watermark rectangle, giving a coarse map; it cannot resolve 1-6% holes on its own. A reasonable use is as an extra input channel or a prior for the segmentation head, not as the final mask (speculation).
- Matched-filter idea (speculation, no paper found): re-embed the known message into the received image (or into the predicted-clean image) with the real embedder to get the expected residual r(x, m), then feed [image, r] or the local normalised correlation between the high-passed image and r to the segmentation network. For PixelSeal/TrustMark the residual is content-adaptive, so correlation must be computed against a residual regenerated from the received image; after a FLUX VAE the residual is low-pass filtered, so a learned filter (network input channel) is safer than a fixed correlator.
- Mask optimisation through the differentiable decoder ("extremal perturbation"-style: optimise a smooth mask M so that decoding M*x + (1-M)*blur(x) still matches the known message with minimal area) was not found applied to watermark localisation. The team's failed occlusion/gradient attribution suggests the same weakness: these decoders pool globally and respond to occluders/blur as out-of-distribution inputs.

### Gaps
- No numbers exist in the literature for any of the global-to-local routes on these three watermarks; all of the above must be validated on the team's synthetic set.
- Attention rollout / linear probes on watermark decoder features: not found.

## 3. Do the three extractors carry local information? Architecture, training augmentations, crop robustness

### Takeaway
PixelSeal's released head is global: spatial mean over the ConvNeXt feature map followed by one linear layer, so "per-pixel bit logits" exist only as an exact linear decomposition at the last stride (not trained to be local), and it was trained with crops but no masks or splicing. TrustMark-Q was trained with only very mild crop/resize (resize scale 0.8-1.0, 244/256 crop) and has a ResNet-50 with a fully connected head. StegaStamp was not verified this session.

### Cited Findings
- videoseal `PixelDecoder.forward`: `upscaled_embedding = self.output_upscaling(image_embeddings)`; `if not self.pixelwise: upscaled_embedding = upscaled_embedding.mean(dim=[-2, -1])`; `preds = self.linear(upscaled_embedding)`. The head is `nn.Linear(embed_dim, nbits+1)` when not pixelwise and a 1x1 `nn.Conv2d(embed_dim, nbits+1)` when pixelwise. Output has 1+nbits channels (one detection/mask channel plus bits). — [videoseal/modules/pixel_decoder.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/videoseal/main/videoseal/modules/pixel_decoder.py)
- Extractor configs: `convnext_tiny` has `pixelwise: False`, `upscale_stages: [1]`, `embed_dim: 768`, `sigmoid_output: False`; a pixelwise variant `convnext_tiny_pw` exists with `upscale_stages: [4, 4, 2]`. No config is literally named "pixelseal"; `convnext_chunky` (non-pixelwise, `proportional_dim: True`) also exists. — [configs/extractor.yaml](https://github.com/facebookresearch/videoseal/blob/main/configs/extractor.yaml)
- `ConvnextExtractor.forward`: `latents = self.convnext(imgs)  # b c h/f w/f`, `masks = self.pixel_decoder(latents)`; docstring output `Bx(1+nbits)xHxW`. — [videoseal/models/extractor.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/videoseal/main/videoseal/models/extractor.py)
- PixelSeal paper: extractor ConvNeXt-v2 Tiny (33.4M params), embedder U-Net (43.8M), 256 bits, model resolution 256x256; training images randomly resized with sides in [256, 768]; the watermark is computed at 256x256, upscaled and multiplied by a full-resolution JND map; augmentations described only as "geometric changes (e.g., cropping, resizing, and rotation), valuemetric adjustments ... and compression (e.g., JPEG)". Masking, padding, splicing, localisation, VAE and diffusion regeneration are not mentioned. Training at fixed 256 only gives Geometric 0.81 / Combined 0.68 bit accuracy vs 0.93 / 0.94 with high-resolution training (SA-1b). — [arXiv 2512.16874](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16874)
- PixelSeal Table 1 bit accuracy (Meta AI images): identity 1.00, valuemetric 0.97, compression 0.98, geometric 0.95, combined 0.91. Fig. 8: JPEG 40 -> 0.96; combined (JPEG + crop 0.71 + brightness 0.5) at JPEG 40 -> 0.86. — [arXiv 2512.16874](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16874)
- Video Seal (arXiv 2412.09492, predecessor): ViT-S extractor, latent map average-pooled then linear to 96 bits; training augmentations (Table 3): crop edge ratio 0.7-1.0, resize edge ratio 0.7-1.5 (each edge independent), rotation +-10 deg, perspective 0.1-0.5, JPEG 40-80, blur kernel 3-17, H.264. No masking/splicing. Geometric-category bit accuracy 0.83 (SA-1b). Frames are resized to 256x256 before extraction. — [arXiv 2412.09492](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.09492)
- TrustMark (arXiv 2311.18297): decoder is a standard ResNet-50 with the last layer replaced by an l-dim sigmoid FC layer. Training geometric noise: random flip (50%), resize scale 0.8-1.0, aspect ratio 3/4-4/3, crop size 244 of 256; no rotation. 15 perturbation sources (JPEG, brightness, contrast, Gaussian blur/noise, motion blur, ...). Resolution scaling: image interpolated to 256x256, residual interpolated back to HxW and added. Bit accuracy clean -> noised: TrustMark-Q 1.00 -> 0.95+-0.09 (CLIC, DIV2K), TrustMark-B 1.00 -> 0.97. TrustMark-RM remover: PSNR 48.48, bit accuracy after removal 0.553. No VAE/diffusion evaluation. — [arXiv 2311.18297](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.18297)
- TrustMark under crop in the 2026 benchmark: degrades, "milder" than the comparison method, no number in the read text. — [arXiv 2609.16832](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16832)

### Inferences
- Crop-robust decoding (sub-crop resized to model input): PixelSeal TRUE with numbers (0.98 at 0.71, 0.81 at 0.45, 0.58 at 0.32); TrustMark-Q UNKNOWN-to-weak (trained only down to ~0.8 scale; the team's own brute-force rectangle search working for TrustMark suggests a sharp-but-usable peak, consistent with weak crop tolerance); StegaStamp UNKNOWN from sources read here, and the team's own measurement (peak ~4 px / ~5% scale wide) says effectively FALSE.
- Because mean and linear commute, PixelSeal's global logits are exactly the spatial mean of `linear(feature[:, :, i, j])`. For a 256x256 input and a stride-32 ConvNeXt that is an 8x8 map (inference: stride 32 is the standard ConvNeXt total stride and `upscale_stages: [1]` adds no upsampling). That is one cell per 64x64 px of a 512 image when the full image is fed, far too coarse for 1-6% regions and blurred by a large receptive field. This is the likely reason "per-position logits of the deepest layer" failed. Options (speculation): (i) feed the image at 512 or 768 so the map becomes 16x16 or 24x24 - the network is fully convolutional, but the watermark then appears at 2-3x the trained scale, so first resample so that the watermark rectangle maps to ~256 px; (ii) use the known message to project features: per-cell score s_ij = sum_k sign(m_k) * logit_k,ij, which is the matched statistic for that decomposition.
- The detection channel (index 0 of the 1+nbits output) exists in the head; whether it was trained with a meaningful target in PixelSeal is not stated in the paper (Video Seal says it does not use a detection bit), so it should not be relied on.
- Since none of the three was trained with splicing/masks, their features have no incentive to be spatially separable; this supports training a separate pixel-space segmenter rather than probing decoder internals.

### Gaps
- Exact PixelSeal training crop/resize ranges and the checkpoint's actual config name (which YAML entry the released PixelSeal card uses): not found in the read pages; check the model card YAML in facebookresearch/videoseal.
- StegaStamp (arXiv 1904.05343): architecture and training (perspective warp, STN) not fetched this session. Unverified recall: 400x400, 100 bits, trained with random perspective warp, blur, colour shift, noise, JPEG; decoder has a spatial transformer; print-scan robustness relied on a separate detector network (BiSeNet) to find the stamp, which is itself a hint that the decoder alone is not translation/scale tolerant.
- TrustMark per-noise crop/resize numbers are only in its Fig. 5 (not readable).

## 4. Robustness to VAE round trips, diffusion regeneration, downscaling, small stretched rectangles

### Takeaway
Published evidence is thin and partly contradictory: WAVES reports StegaStamp fully surviving its VAE regeneration attacks and only mildly affected by diffusion regeneration, while the 2026 local-watermark benchmark reports that SD-pipeline whole-image reconstruction and outpainting reduce payload recovery to chance for all tested methods including TrustMark and PixelSeal. No FLUX-VAE numbers for any of the three watermarks were found.

### Cited Findings
- WAVES (arXiv 2401.08573), StegaStamp, Table 3 (average TPR@0.1%FPR across attack strengths): Regen-VAE 1.000 (never drops below thresholds), Regen-Diff 0.943, Regen-DiffP 0.940, Rinse-2xDiff 0.941, Rinse-4xDiff 0.909; distortions hurt far more: rotation 0.357, resized-crop 0.540. Quote: "The StegaStamp is mildly affected by regenerations, and only by diffusive attacks." — [arXiv 2401.08573](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.08573)
- 2026 benchmark: inpainting through the SD inpainting pipeline removes payload outside the mask too; restoring outside pixels recovers MaskWM evidence to 14.62-17.45; both outpainting variants bring payload recovery "down to random guessing". — [arXiv 2609.16832](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16832)
- PixelSeal paper and TrustMark paper report no VAE or diffusion-regeneration results. — [arXiv 2512.16874](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16874), [arXiv 2311.18297](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.18297)
- MaskMark needed explicit fine-tuning with VAE distortions (SD v1-4, Bmshj18, Cheng20; 20k steps) to reach "near 100%" under VAE attacks, implying it was not robust before. — [arXiv 2504.12739](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12739v2)
- A March 2026 preprint on diffusion editing vs StegaStamp/TrustMark/VINE (arXiv 2603.12949) states its tables are "hypothetical but realistic"; do not use its numbers. A Feb 2026 preprint (arXiv 2602.20680) reports near-zero recovery after diffusion edits for StegaStamp, TrustMark, VINE (search snippet only, not read). — [arXiv 2603.12949](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12949), [arXiv 2602.20680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20680)
- Region size: PixelSeal evidence 0.36 for 0-3% regions vs 27.42 for 40-85% regions when decoded globally. — [arXiv 2609.16832](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16832)
- Resolution: PixelSeal trained on 256-768 px sides with the watermark upscaled from 256; training at fixed 256 costs 0.12-0.26 bit accuracy on geometric/combined attacks. — [arXiv 2512.16874](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16874)

### Inferences
- WAVES' "Regen-VAE" attacks are, to my recollection, learned-compression VAEs (Bmshj18/Cheng20) rather than SD/FLUX latent VAEs (unverified), so "StegaStamp survives VAE" does not directly transfer to a FLUX VAE; FLUX's 16-channel VAE reconstructs more faithfully than SD 1.x's 4-channel VAE, so survival is plausibly better than in the SD-inpainting benchmark (speculation). This must be measured: round-trip 100 clean-watermarked images through the FLUX VAE and record known-message bit accuracy per method; it takes minutes and decides whether decode-based routes are viable at all.
- A watermark stretched into a 60x90 px rectangle is the 256x256 (or 400x400) residual downscaled 3-6x. None of the three papers trains for that: TrustMark trains resize 0.8-1.0, Video Seal 0.7-1.5, PixelSeal up-scales only (256 -> up to 768). Expect high-frequency residual components to be destroyed; decoding after cropping the rectangle and upsampling to model resolution is the only configuration with any support, and for 1-4% regions the published PixelSeal number (evidence 0.36 with global decoding) says the region must be cropped first. No published number for the cropped-and-upsampled case: not found.
- For the mask task this suggests two regimes: big watermarked area with small clean holes (decoder evidence is saturated and uninformative about holes -> pixel-space segmenter needed), and small watermarked rectangles (decoder evidence after zoom is the discriminative signal -> rectangle search with known message, then the segmenter refines).

### Gaps
- FLUX VAE / SDXL VAE round-trip bit accuracy for PixelSeal, TrustMark-Q, StegaStamp: not found.
- 2x downscale bit accuracy for any of the three: not found (per-attack resize curves exist only as figures).
- W-Bench / VINE (ICLR 2025) regeneration and local-editing tables for StegaStamp and TrustMark were surfaced but not read.

## 5. Synchronisation / geometric search to locate a scaled, translated watermarked rectangle with a narrow peak

### Takeaway
Learned synchronisation (SyncSeal) needs its own embedded sync signal and explicitly cannot estimate scale, so it does not apply post hoc; no published blind method was found that recovers translation+scale for an existing StegaStamp/TrustMark/PixelSeal watermark. The narrow-peak search has to be engineered (coarse-to-fine with a smoothed, known-message soft objective, or a learned box regressor).

### Cited Findings
- SyncSeal ("Geometric Image Synchronization with Deep Watermarking", arXiv 2509.15208, Meta FAIR, Sept 2025): an embedder adds a sync watermark and an extractor regresses four corner coordinates (8 values) defining a homography. Trained on crop area 0.3-1.0, rotation +-135 deg, perspective up to 0.3. The paper says the method "cannot predict scale changes". Corner error 7.3 +- 16.4 px on average; crop 0.7 -> 3.8, perspective 0.1 -> 6.6; extraction 12 ms vs 565 ms for WMAR sync and 146 ms for SIFT. With SyncSeal, average bit accuracy TrustMark 0.71 -> 0.97, WAM 0.83 -> 0.99. Code: github.com/facebookresearch/wmar/tree/main/syncseal — [arXiv 2509.15208](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15208v1)
- SyncSeal requires embedding; the only non-embedding baseline (SIFT+RANSAC) needs the original image. Template methods are discussed only qualitatively; Fourier-Mellin is not mentioned. — [arXiv 2509.15208](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15208v1)
- In the 2026 benchmark SyncSeal helps MaskWM (+8 to +12 evidence on shear/translation/perspective/rotation) but lowers PixelSeal's evidence even on clean images and costs 7-19 dB PSNR. — [arXiv 2609.16832](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16832)
- WAVES: StegaStamp average TPR@0.1%FPR under resized-crop 0.540 and rotation 0.357, i.e. it is the geometry-fragile one of the three. — [arXiv 2401.08573](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.08573)

### Inferences (all speculation; no published numbers)
- Smooth the objective instead of hard bit accuracy: use the known-message soft score S(box) = mean_k sign(m_k) * logit_k (or BCE). It varies continuously where bit accuracy plateaus at 0.5, which widens the basin for gradient ascent on the 4 box parameters through `affine_grid`/`grid_sample`. With a 4 px / 5% basin, start from a dense grid (translation step <= 2 px at model resolution, scale step <= 2.5%) restricted by candidate boxes from the segmentation mask, then refine by gradient.
- Use the segmenter to propose the box: the bounding rectangle of the predicted mask gives translation/scale to within a few pixels; the remaining search is then +-8 px and +-10% scale, about 9x9x5x5 ~ 2,000 decodes per image, batched on GPU. For clean-hole cases, the hull of the watermarked area gives the box directly.
- Train a tiny "sync regressor" post hoc: since the embedders are available, generate (image, box) pairs with the real pipeline (stretch to rectangle, edits, FLUX VAE) and regress the 4 box parameters or corner heatmaps from the image; this is SyncSeal's idea without a sync signal and handles scale because the training data contains it.
- StegaStamp's STN may itself be the cause of the narrow peak (it re-warps the input); evaluating the decoder with the STN bypassed/identity while searching, or reading the STN's predicted affine as a correction hint, is cheap to test.
- Fourier-Mellin / log-polar correlation needs a fixed, message-determined template; the three residuals are content-adaptive, so this is unlikely to work except possibly for StegaStamp's residual, which is less content-dependent (speculation).

### Gaps
- No paper found on blind, post hoc recovery of translation+scale for StegaStamp, TrustMark or PixelSeal watermarks.
- WMAR synchronisation details (arXiv 2506.16349) not read beyond SyncSeal's comparison table.
